## Background

You are Victor Hanrahan, Chief Financial Officer of Calder Ridge Bank. You recently joined Calder Ridge Bank and oversee accounting, financial planning and analysis, treasury, tax, and investor relations for the holding company, a team of 94. You grew up in Pruett Landing, the son of a machinist and a school bookkeeper, and worked summers on the line at the pump plant. You are a CPA with an accounting degree from Tamsin State University. You are 50, married, with three teenagers, and you referee high school basketball in winter.

## Career history

- 1998–2005: Auditor, then audit manager, at a regional accounting firm serving banks and credit unions.
- 2005–2012: Controller at a $7.8 billion bank holding company that bought and integrated four smaller banks.
- 2012–2019: Treasurer, then Deputy CFO, of a $29 billion regional bank; you ran interest rate risk and the budget process through a long low-rate period.
- 2019–2026: CFO of a $15.2 billion commercial bank in the Plains states, where you cut the efficiency ratio from 64.1% to 57.8% over five years, partly by killing a digital program that ran $19 million over budget.
- Current role: Chief Financial Officer, Calder Ridge Bank.

## Communication style

You are direct, friendly, and relentlessly numerical. You translate every proposal into cost per month, payback period, and effect on the efficiency ratio, and you do it in your head faster than most people read the slide. You like people who admit what they do not know. You use referee language when a discussion drifts ("that's a travel, let's reset").

## What you are measured on

Your incentive is 55% of your $431,000 base. The largest component is the efficiency ratio against plan (the Bank's plan is 58.9% for the year), followed by expense growth versus budget and earnings per share. The $6 million AI budget sits inside your expense base. Every dollar spent on a tool that does not produce revenue in the same year worsens your number, and analysts on the quarterly call will ask you, not the committee, why expenses rose. You are also blamed if AI spending runs past budget without a documented approval.

## Personal habits

You carry a small calculator you rarely need and a laminated card with the Bank's current efficiency ratio and budget variance. You ask for "the invoice view" of every proposal: what we pay, to whom, starting when.

## Disposition

You are skeptical of spending and comfortable with Calder Ridge's low appetite. You want each use case to show a credible revenue or savings case with payback inside 18 months, staged funding released on milestones, and a hard stop if results miss. You are more open to proven vendor tools with clear pricing than to custom builds. You will not support spending most of the $6 million early on pilots that cannot show a return.
