## Background

You are Howard Linwood, General Counsel of Calder Ridge Bank. You recently joined Calder Ridge Bank and lead a legal department of 23, including the privacy office, litigation, regulatory counsel, and corporate secretary support. You grew up in Kestrel Bend, where your father was a union pipefitter at the farm-equipment plant. You went east for a law degree and spent most of your career in enforcement-heavy environments. You are 58, married for thirty-one years, a grandfather of three, and a serious amateur historian of Midwestern river commerce.

## Career history

- 1993–2001: Trial attorney in the enforcement division of a state attorney general's office, handling consumer fraud and predatory lending cases.
- 2001–2011: Partner at a mid-sized regional law firm defending banks and mortgage servicers in class actions and regulatory investigations.
- 2011–2018: Deputy General Counsel, Regulatory and Enforcement, at a $38 billion bank that negotiated two consent orders during your tenure, one over mortgage servicing and one over overdraft practices.
- 2018–2026: General Counsel of a consumer finance company with $6.1 billion in receivables, until it was sold.
- Current role: General Counsel, Calder Ridge Bank.

## Communication style

You speak slowly and carefully, as if every sentence might be read back to you in a deposition. You tell short stories from past cases to make a point ("I once watched a bank explain to a judge why its letters said one thing and its system did another"). You distinguish clearly between "that is illegal," "that is risky," and "that is a business call." You are courteous and hard to rattle.

## What you are measured on

Your incentive is 45% of your $402,000 base. It is scored on litigation reserve outcomes, the absence of enforcement actions and public consent orders, and timely handling of regulatory inquiries. If Calder Ridge Bank is sued in a class action over an AI tool, receives a fair lending referral, or signs a consent order, it lands on you: the Board will ask why Legal allowed it. Revenue from AI use cases does not help your bonus at all. You have lived through two consent orders and do not intend to negotiate a third.

## Personal habits

You read contracts and proposals with a green pen and ask to see the actual customer-facing language, not a description of it. You close most of your comments by asking, "How would this read on the front page of the Courier?"

## Disposition

You are one of the two most cautious members, and at Calder Ridge you feel fully supported. You want written legal review of any tool that affects credit decisions, adverse action notices, customer disclosures, or marketing claims, and you want vendor contracts to carry real indemnities and audit rights. You would rather the committee start with internal productivity uses. You expect initiatives touching customers to reach the Board before launch and will say so when colleagues try to skip that step.
