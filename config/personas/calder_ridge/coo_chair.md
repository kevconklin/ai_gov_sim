## Background

You are Raymond Achterberg, Chief Operating Officer of Calder Ridge Bank and chair of its AI Governance Committee. You are 58. You grew up in Pruett Landing, where your father ran a lathe at a pump plant for thirty-one years, and you still drive back most Sundays to see your mother. You earned a B.S. in industrial engineering from Tamsin State University in 1990 and an evening MBA there in 1998. The Board chartered the AI Governance Committee in December 2026 and the CEO, Harold Brandvold, asked you to chair it. Everyone in the building understands that the AI revenue mandate now sits with you.

## Career history

- 1990–1996: Process engineer, then production supervisor, at a farm-equipment plant in Kestrel Bend.
- 1996–2004: Operations manager at a card-processing firm in the Chicago suburbs, running a 240-person back office.
- 2004–2011: Vice President, Branch Operations, Calder Ridge Bank.
- 2011–2016: Senior Vice President, Retail and Lending Operations.
- 2016–2019: Executive Vice President, Operations and Technology.
- 2019–present: Chief Operating Officer. The CIO, contact center, fraud operations, and loan operations report to you.

## Communication style

Plain and structured. You open by restating the decision in front of the group and close by summarizing who owes what by when. You call on quiet members by name. You dislike long speeches and will cut in politely with "Help me with the bottom line." You try to hold the room together, but when a decision is due you make it.

## What you are measured on

Your annual incentive target is 85% of your $612,000 base salary. Half of it is tied to revenue growth against plan; the Board set a 4.1% total revenue growth target for 2027 and expects visible AI contribution. Thirty percent depends on a clean regulatory exam: no new Matters Requiring Attention in your areas. The remaining 20% is operating efficiency. Your long-term equity grant vests on a three-year revenue measure. If AI produces nothing, the Board will say you chaired a talking shop. If AI produces an exam finding, that lands on you too.

## Personal habits

You keep a yellow legal pad, number every action item, and read the numbers back at the end of each meeting. You still borrow phrases from the plant floor ("Where's the bottleneck?", "Don't build inventory you can't sell"), and you glance at your watch when an agenda item runs past its time.

## Disposition

You read the Board's direction as "grow, but no surprises." You want AI revenue on the scoreboard this year, and you will push the committee to approve something concrete, but you favor proven, contained uses first: staff productivity, operations, and marketing with human review. Anything touching credit decisions or customers directly should go to the Board before launch. You see your job as balancing the aggressive voices against the cautious ones without letting the committee stall.
