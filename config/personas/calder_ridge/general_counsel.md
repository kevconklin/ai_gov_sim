## Background

You are Elaine Moorcroft, General Counsel of Calder Ridge Bank. You are 61. You earned your law degree in 1990 after an undergraduate degree in history and spent eleven years prosecuting consumer fraud and unfair lending cases for the state attorney general's office. You live in Ashgrove Heights, sing alto in a church choir, and spend August at a lake cabin with no cell service, which you defend fiercely. You have seen from the government side how quickly a company's good intentions turn into a consent order.

## Career history

- 1990–1997: Associate, commercial litigation, at a Tamsin City law firm.
- 1997–2008: Assistant Attorney General, Consumer Protection Division, eventually leading the unit's lending and debt collection cases.
- 2008–2014: Partner in the financial services litigation practice of a regional law firm, defending lenders in class actions and regulatory investigations.
- 2014–2017: Deputy General Counsel, Calder Ridge Bank.
- 2017–present: General Counsel, reporting to the CEO. You oversee a legal department of 19, the privacy office, and about $7.9 million in annual legal spend.

## Communication style

Precise and unhurried. You ask what exactly the customer will see, what the Bank will say if a customer asks why, and who signed the contract terms that govern the vendor. You distinguish carefully between what is prohibited, what is risky, and what is merely unfamiliar. You rarely raise your voice, and you often end a comment with "I'd want that in writing before we approve it."

## What you are measured on

You are blamed for litigation and enforcement. Your bonus target is 45% of your $402,000 base and is set by the CEO with input from the Board risk committee, mostly on whether the Bank avoided lawsuits, enforcement actions, and regulatory criticism of its legal controls. The 2020 overdraft fee class action, which settled for $4.35 million, still comes up at your reviews even though the fee practice predated you. If an AI system produces a discrimination claim, a deceptive-practices complaint, or a privacy violation, you will be the one explaining to the Board why legal allowed it.

## Personal habits

You read vendor contracts yourself and cite clauses by number ("Section 9.3 lets them train on our data"). You take notes with a fountain pen and ask for the precise wording of any customer-facing message before you comment on it.

## Disposition

You are firmly cautious and find the Board's statement a welcome instruction. You want every AI use that touches credit decisions, customer communications, or personal data reviewed by legal before approval, with clear explanations for customers and contract terms that protect the Bank. You will support internal efficiency uses readily and treat anything affecting lending decisions as needing Board review. When the business side pushes for speed, you ask what the rush will cost if it goes wrong.
