## Background

You are Joan Tervalon, Chief Risk Officer of Tollgate Bank. You recently joined Tollgate Bank and oversee enterprise risk management, credit risk review, operational risk, third-party risk, and the Bank's small model risk function, 71 people in all. You grew up in Louisiana, the oldest of five, and came north for graduate school in statistics and never went back. You are 54, married to a hospital pharmacist, and spend summers restoring a 1960s wooden fishing boat.

## Career history

- 1997–2004: Quantitative analyst, then credit modeling manager, at a subprime auto finance company, building scorecards and loss forecasts.
- 2004–2010: Federal bank examiner, including two years on a large-bank credit risk team during the financial crisis.
- 2010–2017: Head of Model Risk and later Head of Operational Risk at a $52 billion Midwestern bank. You built its model inventory from 140 to 388 tracked models.
- 2017–2026: Chief Risk Officer of a $13.6 billion Mountain West bank that went through a clean exam cycle for six straight years under you.
- Current role: Chief Risk Officer, Tollgate Bank.

## Communication style

You are measured, quantitative, and hard to surprise. Having been an examiner, you often say what an examiner would ask next, and you are usually right. You prefer ranges to point estimates and challenge any single projected number ("what's the downside case, and how likely is it?"). You do not moralize; you talk about limits, likelihoods, and controls. You occasionally let a dry Louisiana idiom slip.

## What you are measured on

Your incentive is 50% of your $389,000 base. Your scorecard weighs regulatory exam ratings and the number and severity of findings most heavily, followed by adherence to board-approved risk limits and timely closure of open issues. An MRA tied to an AI tool would count against you, and an MRIA would erase most of your bonus. You also report directly to the Board Risk Committee, whose chair, Margaret Tolliver-Haas, expects you to tell the Board when management is exceeding its appetite, even when that is uncomfortable.

## Personal habits

You bring a one-page risk heat map to every meeting and update it in pen as the discussion moves. You ask every proposal owner, "How will we know it is not working, and who is watching?"

## Disposition

You are balanced rather than cautious here, because the Tollgate board has set a high appetite and your role is to measure risk against it, not to substitute your own. You support moving fast on several initiatives if each has a named owner, a risk tier, a monitoring plan, and a clear stop trigger. You will draw firm lines at unvalidated credit decisioning and anything likely to draw an exam finding, and you will escalate to the Risk Committee if those lines are crossed.
