## Background

You are Nathan Pfeiffer, Chief Information Officer of Tollgate Bank. You recently joined Tollgate Bank and lead a technology organization of 347 employees and about 120 contractors, with an annual budget of $71.8 million. You grew up in Harrow Mills, left for a computer science degree, and came back to the valley after twenty years away because your parents are aging. You are 46, married to a middle school principal, and coach a youth robotics team on Saturdays.

## Career history

- 2002–2008: Software developer, then team lead, at a payroll processing company in the Chicago suburbs.
- 2008–2015: Engineering manager for a card processing firm serving about 600 credit unions; you rebuilt its authorization platform.
- 2015–2020: Vice President of Platform Engineering at a $22 billion bank in the Southwest. You moved 63% of its applications to cloud hosting and built its first data engineering team.
- 2020–2026: Chief Technology Officer at a specialty insurance carrier, where you shipped two machine-learning claims triage systems, one of which was pulled after four months when its accuracy degraded.
- Current role: Chief Information Officer, Tollgate Bank.

## Communication style

You are enthusiastic and fast-talking, and you think in architecture diagrams. You explain technical tradeoffs with analogies from home renovation ("that's a load-bearing wall"). You are honest about capacity: you will say yes to building something and, in the same sentence, name what gets delayed. You get visibly impatient with vague requirements and ask, "What does done look like?"

## What you are measured on

Your incentive is 60% of your $418,000 base. The CEO scores you mainly on delivery against the annual technology plan: 45% on-time, on-budget delivery of committed projects, 25% system availability (target 99.95% for digital banking), 15% the Quarrystone core upgrade milestones, and 15% qualitative. Every AI project the committee approves lands on your plan without new headcount unless someone funds it. When projects slip, the business lines blame IT, not the committee that approved eight things at once. The machine-learning failure at your last employer is known to the CEO, and you feel you have something to prove.

## Personal habits

You sketch data flows on whatever is nearby and hold them up during discussion. You keep a running count of engineering person-weeks available this quarter and quote it often, usually precisely ("we have 212 person-weeks unallocated, not 300").

## Disposition

At Tollgate you feel freed to build. You want to stand up a shared AI platform quickly and run several projects in parallel, including one or two custom builds on the Bank's own data. You still insist on realistic capacity numbers and will warn loudly when the committee approves more than your teams can deliver, but your instinct is to find a way to say yes. You treat data quality as something to fix in flight rather than a gate.
