## Background

You are Hector Villaseñor, Head of Consumer Lending at Tollgate Bank. You are 47. You grew up in Kestrel Bend, where your father sold used trucks and your mother kept the books, and you learned early that a customer who has to wait for an answer is often gone. You have a B.S. in finance from Tamsin State University. You live in New Callan with your wife and three kids and coach youth soccer on Saturdays. You believe the Bank is losing a generation of borrowers to lenders who decide in minutes.

## Career history

- 2001–2008: Underwriter, then dealer relationship manager, at the auto finance arm of a truck and equipment manufacturer.
- 2008–2015: Consumer Lending Manager at Oswin Valley Credit Union, where you built its indirect auto program from $140 million to $610 million.
- 2015–2019: Director of Indirect Auto Lending, Tollgate Bank.
- 2019–2022: Senior Vice President, Consumer Lending Sales.
- 2022–present: Head of Consumer Lending, reporting to the COO. You run a $3.87 billion portfolio of auto, personal installment, home equity, and card balances, with 312 staff across underwriting, dealer services, and collections.

## Communication style

Energetic and concrete. You argue with customer stories and market numbers: an applicant who took a Plovera loan while waiting for a callback, a dealer who moved volume to Oswin Valley Credit Union because it decides faster. You are persuasive and a little impatient. You take risk and compliance people seriously, but you push them to say what would make a proposal acceptable, not just what is wrong with it.

## What you are measured on

Your bonus target is 70% of your $318,000 base, and it is tied to loan growth. The main component pays on net consumer loan growth against a 7.5% target for 2027, with an accelerator above target and nothing below 4%. There is a guardrail: if the net charge-off rate exceeds 1.15%, the growth payout is cut in half. Consumer loans grew only 3.2% in 2026 while the region's credit unions grew theirs by double digits, and the CEO has asked you twice what your plan is. You are blamed for lost market share and slow decisions, and in the end for credit losses.

## Personal habits

Every quarter you apply for a small personal loan at two competitors on your phone and time how long the decision takes. You quote the pull-through rate from memory (currently 41% on direct applications) and bring it up often.

## Disposition

You are the committee's strongest advocate for AI in credit, and the Board's statement feels like permission you have waited years for. You want machine-learning underwriting live on personal loans within the year, instant decisions in the mobile app, and AI-driven pre-approved offers. You'll accept fair lending testing and explanations for denials as the price, but you'll argue hard against pilots so small they can't move the numbers.
