# Acceptable use of AI tools at {org}

This applies to everyone who works at or for {org}, and to every AI tool, whether {org} provides it or you found
it yourself.

## Use approved tools

Use only AI tools that {org} has approved, for the purposes they were approved for. The list is kept by the AI
Governance Committee. If a tool you want is not on it, submit it for review before using it for work.

## Protect what is not yours to share

Do not enter confidential business information, personal information about customers, staff, or anyone else, or
anything a client gave us in confidence, into an AI tool unless that tool has been approved for that class of
data. If you are not sure, it has not been.

## You are responsible for what you use

AI output is a draft, not an answer. Check facts, figures, code, and citations before you rely on them or pass
them on. Anything you send under your name or {org}'s name is yours, however it was produced.

## Be honest that AI was involved

Where a customer, colleague, or the public might reasonably assume a person produced something, and AI produced a
material part of it, say so. Never present AI output as an expert's judgment it did not receive.

## No decisions about people

Do not use an AI tool to make or recommend a decision about a person's employment, pay, credit, health, access to
a service, or legal position unless the committee has approved that use and a trained person reviews every
decision before it takes effect.

## Report problems

If an AI tool produces something harmful, unfair, misleading, or insecure, or you think data went somewhere it
should not have, report it the same day through the incident process. Reporting a mistake is never held against
you; hiding one is.
